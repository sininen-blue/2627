What is an integrated circuit?

- Many gates fabricated together on one piece of silicon
- A single gate sold as a standalone component
- A socket that holds a silicon die in place
- A row of pins used to connect two chips

---

Why does an integrated circuit come inside a plastic or ceramic package?

- To protect the tiny silicon die and give it connection points
- To increase the number of transistors on the die
- To convert input signals into output signals
- To reduce the number of pins the chip requires

---

What do the pins extending from an IC package do?

- Carry input signals, output signals, power, and ground
- Hold the silicon die in place inside the package
- Convert transistor signals into logic gate outputs
- Determine how many transistors fit on the die

---

Which package style is typically used for small, simple ICs with only a handful of gates?

- DIP, with two rows of pins along the long edges
- PGA, with pins arranged in a grid on the underside
- LGA, with flat metal contact pads instead of pins
- A single central pin surrounded by a socket

---

How does a PGA package connect to the rest of a circuit?

- Pins arranged in a grid on the underside plug into a socket
- Two rows of pins along the long edges plug into a breadboard
- Flat metal contact pads press against pins in a socket
- A single row of pins runs along one edge of the chip

---

How does an LGA package differ from a PGA package?

- LGA uses flat metal contact pads with pins living in the socket
- LGA uses two rows of pins along the long edges
- LGA has fewer connection points than a DIP package
- LGA requires no silicon die inside the package

---

Why do larger, more complex ICs like CPUs use denser packaging styles such as PGA or LGA?

- They need far more connections than a two-row package offers
- They contain far fewer transistors than simple ICs
- They use dedicated power and ground pins exclusively
- They are fabricated inside a thinner protective casing

How many connections does each 2-input NAND gate need?

- 3 connections, for 2 inputs and 1 output
- 2 connections, for 1 input and 1 output
- 4 connections, for 2 inputs and 2 outputs
- 1 connection, shared for input and output

---

Why must raw gate inputs and outputs be routed through far fewer chip pins?

- Packaging every gate individually would require billions of pins
- Gates produce signals through a separate shared connection instead
- Silicon dies are too large to fit inside a package
- Power and ground pins already use all available space
